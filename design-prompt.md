# Design brief

_Roll: Modernist (#15, "clean lines, functional beauty, timeless"), picked by `pick_style.py`; Swiss/International, Tech Forward and Material were skipped as recent. Kept rather than overridden: Modernism's "form follows function" suits an app whose whole job is removing decisions, and its geometry (circles, half-circles, colour blocks) gives a sunny-side-up egg a natural home._

Design a two-page site in the Modernist style for Yolkie, a pre-launch cooking app that gets a hungry, tired person to a finished plate at any meal of the day, breakfast, lunch, dinner or a snack: a landing page that tells the story, and a working demo that lets visitors feel it in their own hands. Modernism here should feel like a well-designed kitchen at golden hour: everything has a place, every shape has a job, and the room is warm because the light is, not because it is cluttered. Visitors should arrive feeling instantly lighter, as if someone had cleared the counter for them. The layout is a confident grid of flat colour fields and clean rules, with one recurring geometric motif, the circle, which is also the yolk, the plate, the timer and the dot that says "you are here". Colour comes in bold, deliberate blocks: deep plum as the structural colour, yolk-yellow as the one thing your eye goes to, basil green reserved for "done", so the page feels cheerful and edible rather than austere.

The philosophy is functional kindness: a design that does the deciding so the visitor doesn't have to, and is honest about what it is. Typography should feel like a friendly poster from a mid-century kitchen campaign: a heavy, slightly condensed grotesque for headlines that sits tight and speaks plainly, and a soft, open sans for everything you read or tap. Motion should feel mechanical in the best sense, precise and satisfying, like a dial clicking into place: blocks slide along the grid's axes, circles scale up from their centre, numbers tick, and Yolkie the egg is the one thing allowed to wobble, sigh and celebrate. The landing page runs from recognition (the 7:48 pm couch, the lonely lemon) through relief (four plain, numbered steps), to delight (the small, clever features and the ad), and ends on one obvious invitation; the demo page then turns that promise into a sequence of single, large decisions that end on a finished plate.

Take inspiration from the Bauhaus kitchen and its belief that daily chores deserve good design, from mid-century cookbook illustration with its flat shapes and printed-paper colours, from enamel kitchen timers and railway-station signage where one big number tells you what to do now. Think of the calm of a tidy mise en place, the satisfaction of a well-labelled spice drawer, and the wit of modernist posters that made a single circle say everything. These references should give the site clarity without coldness and order without strictness, so that every screen feels like a clean plate with one good idea on it, and a small, sleepy egg making sure nobody takes it too seriously.

_Update: the app was renamed from Tonight to Yolkie (after its mascot) and widened from dinner to every meal. The style, palette and look are unchanged._

## Pages

- **Home (`index.html`)**: the main journey described above, ending on "Get early access".
- **Try it (`try.html`)**: the practical room in the same building. One decision per card, big type, big tap targets, the grid turning into a step-by-step track; Yolkie's face carries the mood from sleepy to celebrating, and it ends on "Get early access" too.
- **Help (`help.html`)**: the reference shelf in the same building. Calm, scannable and quiet: numbered sections that fold away like labelled drawers, a search that narrows them as you type, and one “Try it” on every guide that walks you straight back into the demo. Yolkie looks on, cheerful, and it ends on “Try the demo”.
